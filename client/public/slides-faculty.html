MORITZ COLLEGE OF LAW 1

What is vibe coding?

A way for non-technical users to create software or apps by describing what they want in natural language, while AI takes care of generating the code behind the scenes.

"There's a new kind of coding I call 'vibe coding', where you fully give in to the vibes, embrace exponentials, and forget the code even exists."

— Andrej Karpathy, Feb 2025

You talk to AI. AI writes code. You get a working tool.

MORITZ COLLEGE OF LAW 2

Why this matters for your teaching

  • The jump from "I can automate this for myself" to "I can build this for my students" is huge
  • AI-assisted development collapsed the gap between "that's a great idea" and "I'm using it in class tomorrow"
  • Vibe-coding replaced the paper handout — when the subject is technology, the medium should be too
  • The people who understand the problem best should be the ones building the solution

TokenExplorer

Students manipulate temperature settings and watch probability distributions shift in real time.

Key insight: Once students see that LLMs are statistical prediction engines generating likely text, not true text, everything else clicks.

MORITZ COLLEGE OF LAW 5

Citation Hallucination Game

Competitive team exercise: students create hallucinated citations, then try to catch another team's fakes under time pressure.

Key insight: The lesson isn't "can you catch every error" but "given limited time, which errors do you prioritize?"

MORITZ COLLEGE OF LAW 7

What vibe-coded apps can & can't do

✓ Your app CAN

  • Follow a decision tree (if/then logic)
  • Calculate deadlines, fees, or scores
  • Look things up in data you provide
  • Display information based on user choices
  • Enforce rules and flag errors

✗ Your app CANNOT

  • Read and interpret a document
  • Summarize or paraphrase text
  • Answer open-ended questions
  • Make judgment calls
  • Do anything requiring "understanding"

The index card test: If you could write every possible answer on index cards ahead of time (even with help from AI!), your app can handle it. If you'd need a smart person to figure out the answer after you've handed them over — it can't.

MORITZ COLLEGE OF LAW 8

Security & ethics

  • Do not enter client data into vibe-coded apps — these tools are not vetted for confidentiality
  • Secure deployment may require IT help or technical know-how
  • Ethical duties still apply — no legal advice, maintain confidentiality, no misleading advertising
  • Treat vibe-coded tools as internal aids, not client-facing products (at least for now)
  • Faculty should vet tools before classroom use — AI-generated code may contain bugs or security issues
MORITZ COLLEGE OF LAW 9

How to get started

  • Pick the smallest version of your idea
  • Open Gemini Canvas (free with your university Google account)
  • Describe what you want in plain language
  • Iterate — the first version is just the start
  • Share what you build with colleagues

You don't need to be a coder. You need to describe what you want clearly enough for AI to build it.

  • Lovable — full-stack app builder
  • Bolt.new — build and deploy in the browser
