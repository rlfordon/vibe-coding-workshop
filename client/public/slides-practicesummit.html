What is vibe coding?

A way for non-technical people to create software by describing what they want in natural language, while AI generates the code behind the scenes.

"There's a new kind of coding I call 'vibe coding', where you fully give in to the vibes, embrace exponentials, and forget the code even exists."

— Andrej Karpathy, Feb 2025

You describe what you need. AI writes the code. You get a working tool.

AI Legal Practice Summit 2026

Why vibe code?

  • You're the domain expert. You know your practice, your clients, your workflows. That expertise is the hard part — the coding is now the easy part.
  • Off-the-shelf doesn't solve your problem. No vendor builds software for your specific intake process, your jurisdiction's deadlines, or your niche practice area.
  • The cost of experimenting is near zero. Build a prototype in an afternoon. Try it. Toss it or iterate. No developer, no budget request, no six-month timeline.
  • You understand the landscape better. If you can build a rough version in an hour, you know what commercial tools are actually doing — and what to look for (or ignore) when vendors pitch you. Your prototype becomes the spec.

Security & Ethics

  • Confidentiality (Rule 1.6) — do not enter client data into AI tools or vibe-coded apps unless you've vetted where that data goes
  • Competence (Rule 1.1) — you don't need to understand the code, but you do need to understand what the tool does and verify it works correctly
  • Supervision (Rules 5.1/5.3) — if staff or associates use a tool you built, you're responsible for ensuring it performs as intended
  • Data residency — when you use Gemini or AI Studio, your prompts and code are processed on Google's servers. Know what your tool's terms of service say about data retention

Before you build, ask: "what data will this touch and who will have access?" A fee calculator with no client data is very different from an intake form that collects PII — and that answer shapes every later choice.

Step 2: Choose a tool

Gemini Canvas

  • Free with any Google account
  • Describe what you want, get a live preview
  • Builds fast — first version in minutes
  • Great for calculators, decision trees, forms

Recommended for today. You'll have a working tool in the session.

Index card test: If every possible answer could fit on index cards, your app can handle it. Anything that needs judgment — it can't.

Google AI Studio

  • Free with any Google account
  • Has a Build tab for creating apps
  • Builds are slower (10+ minutes)
  • Can incorporate AI — document review, analysis, summarization

More powerful, but slower. You may not finish in the session.

Other options: ChatGPT has Canvas and Claude has Artifacts — both can generate apps the same way. Use whatever you're comfortable with.

AI Legal Practice Summit 2026

Step 3: Plan with AI

Don't just say "build me an app." Have a conversation first.

  • Tell Gemini what problem you want to solve and who it's for
  • Let it ask you questions — answer honestly (even "I'm not sure" is fine)
  • Go back and forth until you both understand what you're building
  • Then tell it to start building

Your prompt template ends with: "Before you start building, ask me a few questions about who this is for and what their experience should be like."

Working from a PDF? Paste the text, not the file. LLMs handle raw text much better than document structure. If you need to convert, open a separate AI chat, drop in the PDF, and ask for plain text you can bring back over.

Step 4: Build it

  • Go to the Build tab — copy the prompt template or pick an idea card
  • Paste the prompt into Gemini and plan together
  • When you're ready to build, click "Canvas" under Tools
  • Test it — click through, try different inputs
  • Don't worry about getting it perfect on the first try

No Canvas button? That's OK — ask Gemini to write the code, then copy and paste it into the Preview tab on this site to see it run.

Step 5: Iterate

The first version is just the start. The real skill is the conversation you have with AI after that.

Professional polish "Make this look like something I'd send to a client"
Edge cases "What if someone enters [unusual input]? Handle that gracefully"
Explanation "Add a short explanation after each result so the user understands why"
Security check "What best practices should I follow before sharing this?"
Mobile "Make this work well on a phone screen"
Export "Add a button to download the results as a PDF"

Step 6: Deploy

Before you share it, ask: what data does it touch? A calculator with no personal info is very different from an intake form.

Pick a path

  • Share the Canvas link — click Share in Canvas, send the URL (fastest)
  • Run locally — double-click the HTML file, or email it to someone to open in their browser
  • Netlify Drop — drag a folder, get a live URL in 30 seconds
  • GitHub Pages — free, permanent URL you can keep updating

Before going public

  • If it touches client info — keep it local or behind an internal share
  • If it collects PII or goes client-facing — loop in IT/security for HTTPS, access controls, data handling
  • Don't paste real client facts into Gemini while building (Rule 1.6)

What Canvas apps can & can't do

Your app CAN

  • Follow a decision tree (if/then logic)
  • Calculate deadlines, fees, or scores
  • Look things up in data you provide
  • Display information based on user choices
  • Enforce rules and flag errors

Your app CANNOT

  • Read and interpret a document
  • Summarize or paraphrase text
  • Answer open-ended questions
  • Make judgment calls
  • Do anything requiring "understanding"

The index card test: If you could write every possible answer on index cards ahead of time, your app can handle it. If you'd need a smart person to figure out the answer — it can't. (For that, try Google AI Studio.)

Tools to keep building

You don't need to stop after today. Two paths, depending on how much setup you want.

One-stop platforms

Build and deploy in one place — all in the browser.

  • Replit — vibe-code and deploy in the browser; no local setup
  • Lovable — full web app from a prompt, one-click deploy
  • Bolt.new — similar browser-based full-stack builder

Developer stack

Maximum flexibility — requires installing a few tools.

  • Claude Code (Anthropic) — command-line AI agent; handles multi-file projects
  • GitHub — free code hosting with version history
  • Render — free-tier web hosting that redeploys on push from GitHub
